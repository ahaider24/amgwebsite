## Dimensions, Standard size

Use Standard (3.5 x 2.0 in), not Moo Size. Standard is what every wallet, card
holder and Rolodex in the United States is built around, and a nonstandard card
reads as a novelty rather than as premium.

| Area | Inches | At 300 DPI |
|---|---|---|
| Bleed | 3.66 x 2.16 | 1098 x 648 px |
| Trim | 3.5 x 2.0 | 1050 x 600 px |
| Safe | 3.34 x 1.84 | 1002 x 552 px |

That is 0.08 in of bleed on every side and 0.08 in of safe margin inside the
trim. Note this is roughly 2mm of bleed, NOT the 3mm a generic print brief
assumes. Artwork built to 3mm bleed will be trimmed wrong.

Moo publishes Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign and JPEG templates. Prefer their
template over a hand-built artboard.

## The seam

Luxe's defining feature is a coloured layer visible on the card edge. Eight
options: Raven Black, Chilli Red, Ocean Blue, Polar White, Forest Green, Sunny
Yellow, Tiger Orange, Light Pink.

**Recommendation: Tiger Orange.**

Ocean Blue is the same mistake the old avatar made. Blue appears in this brand
only as the CTA button gradient (#3CB8DE to #2596BE to #1A5E92). It is not a
brand colour. The brand is warm paper, ink, and orange #FF4D1C. A blue seam
would put the one colour that is not the brand on the most tactile edge of the
most personal asset.

Tiger Orange carries the accent into a physical property of the object. When
someone holds the card edge-on, they see the brand colour as a material fact
rather than as printed ink.

Design consequence: with an orange seam, do NOT flood the faces with orange as
well. The seam becomes the accent. Faces should be ink or paper, with orange
used once, or not at all, on the face. Redundant orange makes the seam invisible
as a feature.

## Print considerations to respect

**Uncoated Mohawk Superfine absorbs ink.** Colours print noticeably duller and
darker than they appear on screen. #FF4D1C is a bright RGB orange that already
sits outside CMYK gamut; on uncoated stock it will land duller still. Do not
chase the screen value. Either accept the shift, or choose ink and paper for the
faces and let the physical seam carry the true orange, which does not go through
the CMYK process at all.

**Uncoated is writable.** That is a real feature for someone handing cards to
contractors on a job site. Leave usable white space on the back rather than
flooding it.
