# Moo Luxe card: locked decisions

Amir, 2026-09-18.

## Seam: Tiger Orange

Confirmed. His words: "it's pretty cool to use the tiger orange it will stand out
more than my current one."

Consequence for the artwork: the seam becomes the only orange in the object if
the faces are ink. That is the stronger design and it sidesteps CMYK entirely for
the faces, since the seam is a physical layer of coloured paper rather than
printed ink. Do not flood the faces with orange as well or the seam stops
reading as a feature.

## Mixed orientation, front landscape and back portrait

Amir: "it would be cool to have one side be landscape and the other be vertical,
breaks up the pattern."

Correct instinct and printers handle it routinely. One rule governs it:

**The artwork still ships in the same landscape bleed box. Only the composition
is rotated inside it.** Do not deliver a portrait-shaped file. The card trims to
3.5 x 2.0 in either way; what changes is that the reader turns the card ninety
degrees to read the back. Build the back design rotated within the 3.66 x 2.16 in
bleed artboard, so the type runs along the short edge.

Design consequences worth respecting:

- Portrait composition on a 2.0 in tall face gives you a narrow column. Set less,
  not more. One idea per face.
- The spark artwork is a horizontal gesture, two hands reaching across. Rotating
  it ninety degrees makes it a vertical reach, which reads differently and may
  read better, but it must be checked by eye rather than assumed.
- Whatever anchors the portrait face should sit away from the seam edge so the
  coloured seam is not competing with type at the same edge.

## Faces

Front landscape: the wordmark lockup and contact details.
Back portrait: the spark, with the positioning line.

Ink ground on both is the recommendation, so the Tiger Orange seam carries the

## Unchanged constraints

Moo Luxe Standard. Bleed 3.66 x 2.16 in, trim 3.5 x 2.0, safe 3.34 x 1.84, at
300 DPI. That is 0.08 in of bleed, roughly 2mm, not 3mm.
